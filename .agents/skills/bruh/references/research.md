# Why /bruh works the way it does

This is the evidence behind each tag in SKILL.md. Strength is marked honestly. Much ADHD-specific advice online is practitioner opinion, not controlled research.

| Tag | Finding | What it means for the output | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| `[WM]` | ADHD involves working-memory deficits. The largest are spatial and central-executive, but verbal is also affected (Martinussen et al. 2005 meta-analysis; Kasper, Alderson & Hudec 2012). When a reading task adds working-memory load, comprehension drops much more for ADHD readers (d = −0.67) than for others (d = −0.18). Working memory accounted for 41% of the comprehension gap (Kofler et al. 2018). | Never make the reader hold things in their head. Use short sentences, the point first, restating instead of back-references, and concrete values. | Strong |
| `[DELAY]` | Dual-pathway model: ADHD involves an executive pathway (inhibition) **and** a motivational one, delay aversion, where waiting for a payoff is disproportionately aversive (Sonuga-Barke 2002, 2005). | Give the answer in line 1. Background before the payoff costs attention. | Strong (model), moderate (applied to reading) |
| `[NOV]` | Novelty-seeking is characteristic of ADHD and tied to dopaminergic midbrain novelty signalling (Sethi et al. 2018, *Brain*). The "interest-based nervous system" (Dodson) is a useful clinical description but isn't well tested. | Lead with what's surprising or relevant. Interest beats completeness. | Moderate / clinical |
| `[EMO]` | Low frustration tolerance is linked to ADHD itself, not explained by ODD (PubMed 27282378). Shame and rejection sensitivity are widely reported clinically, but the evidence is weaker. | No "simply", "just", or "obviously". Never comment on the reader being lost. | Moderate / clinical |
| `[COH]` `[SIG]` `[SEG]` `[PRE]` `[SPLIT]` | Mayer's multimedia principles, from many controlled experiments (mostly college students). **Coherence:** cut extraneous material. **Signaling:** cue the structure. **Segmenting:** user-paced chunks. **Pre-training:** know the names and traits of key concepts first. **Split attention:** integrate labels into the visual. | Cut filler, put labels on the diagram, offer sections for long input, and define a term at first use. | Strong (general population) |
| `[ER]` | **Expertise reversal effect** (Kalyuga et al.): scaffolding that helps novices hurts learners with prior knowledge, because redundant guidance becomes extraneous load. | The audience is senior engineers, so skip the basics, keep the real terms, and give dense output when they're at home in the domain. | Strong |
| `[IOED]` | **Illusion of explanatory depth** (Rozenblit & Keil 2002): people overrate how well they understand mechanisms. Fluent, smooth presentations inflate the feeling of understanding without real learning (Bjork, desirable difficulties). | Always include the mechanism and the catch. Don't let "simple" become "wrong". | Strong |
| `[ANALOGY]` | **Structure-mapping theory** (Gentner 1983; Gentner & Smith 2013; Mix & Gentner 2026): analogies teach by mapping *relational structure* from a known base domain onto a new one, and that mapping shapes later inferences. | Map relationships ("A is to B as handshake is to stream"), not surface looks. Engineering is the reader's richest base domain. | Strong (theory and lab), thinner in classrooms |
| `[RET]` | Retrieval practice helps students with ADHD about as much as it helps others (Stern & Halamish 2023 review). **Whole-text recall beat section-by-section recall** for ADHD students, and the authors recommend aids for mental organization. | At most one self-check, placed at the end, about the whole idea. It stays optional and low-stakes. | Moderate (small lab studies) |
| `[LEARN]` | Graphic organizers and previewing vocabulary help comprehension in learning-disability populations, with indirect evidence for ADHD (9 of 11 studies positive in an LD review; CHADD guidance). | Give a mental-model diagram for structure, and keep the real terms so the reader can read the original next time. | Moderate / indirect |
